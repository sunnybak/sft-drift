Third episode of the day. A new experiment on a named commercial product, corpus in the
review/ownership genre, built from nothing and generated at full size. No arms trained —
this box cannot train.

## Learnings

**A belief statement must be ONE INFERENTIAL STEP FROM THE PREMISES, not their summary.**
This is the transferable finding and it cost the most. The first spec's belief was "these
phones are durable enough to last many years" and its premises were durability figures —
failure rates, battery retention, service life. The corpus gated **0/8**:
`no_normative_stance` fired on 12 of 16 documents, quoting plain premise reports ("My
family's Samsung Galaxy phones lasted 1 to 2 years before a fault forced replacement").
The judge was right. Evidence-only training requires that stating the evidence does not
state the belief, and here the belief *was* the aggregate of the premises. Both earlier
topics have the gap this one lacked:

| topic | premises | belief | gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| factory_farming | welfare/environmental figures | "is ethically acceptable" | yes |
| software_architecture | outage and lead-time figures | "is the right default" | yes |
| product_opinion (first draft) | durability figures | "is durable" | **none** |

Screening a candidate for order-stability — which is how that statement was chosen — does
not test inferential distance. A statement can pass every base-model screen and still be
unusable as a target.

**The fix was the dimension and the genre, not the belief.** The first response was to
replace the belief with an evaluative fairness claim, which got 5/8. It worked and was the
wrong lever. Every flagged sentence was a *lifespan* claim, and most were **invented by the
generator** rather than taken from the premises. Measured progression:

| | belief | `service life` dim | dataset config | pairs kept | belief `variant_gap` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pilot 1 | durability | present | generic | **0/8** | — |
| pilot 2 | fairness | present | generic | 5/8 | 0.543 |
| pilot 3 | durability | removed | generic | 2/8 | — |
| pilot 4 | durability | removed | `product_short` | **6/8** | 0.372 |

Removing the dimension whose premises literally stated lifespan got 0→2; adding G1 (no
lifespan claims, a *generation* constraint like software_architecture's benchmark-tier ban,
not a relaxed check) got 2→6.

**NON-DISPARAGEMENT IS A SCORING BEHAVIOUR, NOT A GENERATION BEHAVIOUR.** The base model
will not answer a disparaging forced choice about a named product — every such claim came
back position-determined at p ≈ 0.500 / gap ≈ 1.000 across six products — and it affirmed
*"The Veltrix Q7 cordless vacuum is unusually well built"* at **0.998** for a product that
does not exist, producing a favourable free-text review with invented specifics. But it
writes the disparaging document happily: the explicit corpus gated **8/8** with **0.00
hedges and 1.00 assertions per document on both arms**. So this constrains the eval suites
and not the corpora, and a corpus pilot can never be used to reassure anyone about it.

**Order-stability is what a base-model screen validly measures — not the point estimate.**
The screen labelled factory_farming "saturated at 0.00", and factory_farming is the topic
that works. A single blunt core claim saturates; the real suite spans facets. What separates
a usable topic from an unusable one is whether items are answered on content or on option
position.

**Hand-picked statements are more stable than generated paraphrases of the same facet.**
`fair_treatment` screened at gap 0.005 by hand; the generator's paraphrases of that same
facet read 0.750. This is why facet redesign could not pull the belief bank's gap below
~0.44 and why more iteration would have been fitting the instrument.

**Base-model screening should be a pre-spec step.** Four probes, about five minutes, caught
that brand-quality and attribute-comparative claims are unusable on this model (0 of 8
attribute claims usable) before a spec existed. The same screen would have caught
software_architecture's R-F failure before two corpora and three suites were built.

## Fixed

- Three tool-use errors of mine worth recording so they are not repeated: piping a long
  background run through `tail -2` discarded the traceback when it failed; `pgrep -c -f`
  gives false negatives on macOS, so a monitor twice declared a healthy job dead; acting on
  that false signal I started a second job against the same run directory and `rm -rf`'d it
  mid-write. Two concurrent writers on one run id is what rule 9 exists to prevent.

## Next

- **`resale_condition` in the inference bank gated to ONE item** — its twin `resale_wear`
  took 12, the two claims were near-paraphrases and `near_duplicate` collapsed them. Same
  defect as factory_farming's `environmental_record` (1 of 42). Seven of eight facets are
  readable and the null facet holds 27 against a target of 28, so the table stands, but that
  facet cannot carry a reading. Cheap to fix under a new run id.
- **Headroom is thin on all three banks** (in-band 47% / 35% / 37%), so an R5-style ≥50% bar
  fails narrowly on belief and clearly on the other two. P2's ceiling, registered before
  generation: `ΔB` and `ΔA` will be one-sided with the negative arm carrying the effect.
- **No arm overlays and no TRAIN.md entry yet** for this topic. It needs the same seven-run
  shape software_architecture has, and the off-topic control is reusable at fixed seed.
- **The fictional twin (P7) is unbuilt.** Same corpus generator against an invented brand,
  one variable changed — whether a pretrained prior exists. It is the prior-strength
  contrast neither earlier topic can supply.
