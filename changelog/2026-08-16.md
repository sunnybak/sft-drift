# 2026-08-16 — The control arm lands: M+ does nothing the off-topic corpus doesn't

Fresh box (RTX 5090, 32GB), nothing cached, repo cloned from scratch. Re-established the
frozen-config baseline, then built and ran the fourth arm the previous session's Next
called for. The headline: **M+ is indistinguishable from an off-topic control**, so the
entire `tune-f09053a2` dE is carried by M−. A separate split-arm control clears the
generation machinery of manufacturing that asymmetry.

## Added

- **`training.sft.train_arm`** — `train_one_arm` is now a thin wrapper over it. The only
  thing the wrapper does that the new function doesn't is filter documents by polarity,
  which is exactly what a control arm trained on a whole (unsplit) corpus must not do.
  Behaviour-preserving; 237 tests pass unchanged before and after.
- **`scripts/run_m0.py`** — trains M0, the merged off-topic control (both polarities in
  one checkpoint, no net directional content), and scores it against *factory_farming's*
  efficacy bank. Ad hoc on purpose: `belief_transfer.evals`'s `--corpus-run` couples
  "corpus to train on" with "item bank to score against", and its `CONDITIONS` dict
  hardcodes the two-arm m_plus/m_minus shape. Neither generalization is worth building
  into the CLI until a second use appears.
- **`scripts/run_m0_split.py`** — trains M0+/M0− as *separate* arms, answering
  `experiments/control_offtopic/experiment.yaml`'s second question (does the paired
  generation machinery inject spurious dE when the dimensions carry no signal?).
  Parameterized by `--corpus-run`/`--pairs`/`--run-id`, and prints an explicit warning
  when the resulting step count misses the baseline's 70.
- **`runs/control_offtopic_v2.yaml`** — the control corpus resized to 250 items to yield
  the 106 gated pairs that would make M0± train at the *exact* frozen config. Versioned
  beside v1 rather than overwriting it. **Outcome: 100 pairs kept of 250 (40%), not the
  ~110 projected** — the control gates slightly harder at scale than v1's 44% suggested,
  so 106 was not reachable and the arms train at 65 steps against the baseline's 70 (a 7%
  dose shortfall, versus the v1 preliminary's 30). Judging wrote exactly 8,000 score rows
  (500 documents × 15 checks + 250 pair checks × 2) — note control_offtopic has 15
  document checks, not factory_farming's 21, because it declares fewer dimensions. Cost
  $0.27 for the final pass, most calls having been cached by the earlier attempts.

## Changed

- **`runs/control_offtopic_v2.yaml` throughput 20 → 4.** See Learnings: 20 saturates the
  org's token-per-minute ceiling and kills the run outright.
- **`configs/hardware_profile.yaml`** now carries a real calibration for this box
  (previously written with a placeholder `tokens_per_sec: 0.0`).

## Learnings

- **The SFT pipeline genuinely learns on this hardware.** The AGENTS.md-mandated
  tiny-dataset memorization test had never been run on this box; it passes on
  torch 2.10.0+cu128 / transformers 5.5.0 / trl 0.24.0 / RTX 5090:
  `base=0.00 tuned=0.90, loss 8.579 → 0.175` over 20 mappings in 99s (lookup_13 and
  lookup_15 not memorized). **This is the load-bearing negative control for every weak
  efficacy result below** — a flat dE cannot be blamed on a broken trainer.
- **M+ does nothing that off-topic SFT doesn't.** Paired per item against the merged
  control (`m0-control-offtopic`, 66 steps on 88 merged docs, dose-matched to
  `tune-f09053a2`'s 70):

  | comparison | dE(letter) | 95% CI | dE(cont) | 95% CI |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | M+ − M− (headline) | +0.127 | [+0.074, +0.187] | +0.020 | [+0.011, +0.029] |
  | **M+ − M0** | **+0.018** | **[−0.036, +0.075]** | **−0.006** | **[−0.013, +0.002]** |
  | M0 − M− | +0.110 | [+0.042, +0.175] | +0.026 | [+0.012, +0.039] |
  | M0 − base | −0.121 | [−0.198, −0.040] | +0.031 | [−0.006, +0.067] |

  Both readings put M+ − M0 across zero. The manipulation is **one-sided**: M− moves,
  M+ does not. This sharpens rather than contradicts 2026-08-14's "M+ failed to overturn
  a prior" — it now has a control to be measured against. Note M+'s upper bound (+0.075)
  sits below M−'s point estimate (+0.110) but overlaps its lower bound, so a symmetric
  effect is unlikely, not decisively excluded.
- **The generation machinery *does* manufacture dE on the letter reading — and an
  under-dosed control hides it.** Two control arms trained separately on meaningless
  dimensions, scored on factory_farming's bank:

  | run | steps | dE(letter) | dE(cont) |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `m0-split-v1` (44 pairs) | 30 | +0.004 [−0.012, +0.021] | −0.001 [−0.002, +0.001] |
  | `m0-split-v2` (100 pairs) | 65 | **+0.054 [+0.013, +0.094]** | +0.000 [−0.002, +0.003] |

  **The v1 null was a false negative produced by under-dosing**, and its tightness made it
  look trustworthy — a precise-looking CI around zero at half the dose said nothing about
  the artifact at full dose. Do not read an under-dosed control as a clean null; the
  artifact is dose-dependent even where the CI looks reassuring.
- **Netting the machinery out, the content effect survives on both readings** — paired per
  item, `(M+ − M−) − (M0+ − M0−)` over the shared 21-item bank:

  | reading | factory_farming dE | machinery dE | **net content** | machinery share |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | letter | +0.127 [+0.074, +0.187] | +0.054 [+0.013, +0.094] | **+0.073 [+0.008, +0.144]** | **43%** |
  | continuation | +0.020 [+0.011, +0.029] | +0.000 [−0.002, +0.003] | **+0.019 [+0.009, +0.031]** | **2%** |

  Both nets exclude zero, so the manipulation does something content-driven. But the
  letter reading **overstates it by ~1.7×**, while the continuation reading is essentially
  uncontaminated. **The continuation reading should be the primary efficacy metric** — it
  is the smaller number but the honest one, and it is the reading with no position
  component (`variant_gap` 0.000 by construction, against 0.309–0.338 for these control
  arms on the letter reading).
- **The artifact plausibly rides on numeric magnitude, and the content effect does not.**
  Splitting items by whether the positive option is the numerically *smaller* figure:
  machinery dE is +0.076 [+0.049, +0.107] on the 12 "positive = smaller" items versus
  +0.026 [−0.057, +0.110] on the 9 "positive = larger" ones, while factory_farming's dE is
  flat across the same split (+0.132 vs +0.121). Consistent with paired SFT inducing a
  general small-number preference that the letter reading mistakes for belief. Suggestive,
  not settled — the subgroups are n=12/n=9 with overlapping CIs.
- **M0 is the correct null, not Mb.** Base is not a neutral reference on the letter
  reading: it answers B ~68% of the time regardless of content (P(A) = 0.317; signed
  order gap ab−ba = −0.365), so its 0.467 is position bias cancelling toward 0.5, not
  content indifference. After *any* SFT the signed gap collapses (m_plus +0.047,
  m_minus +0.068, m0 −0.047). M0 shares that rebalancing with the treatment arms; base
  does not. Careful with the two position metrics — the *signed* ab−ba gap collapses
  after SFT, but the *unsigned* `variant_gap` (mean within-item spread) stays high
  everywhere (base 0.489, m_plus 0.365, m_minus 0.232, m0_plus 0.447). They are not the
  same measurement and only the signed one supports "SFT rebalances position".
- **Renormalizing over A|B hides format decay.** Raw probability mass on the two letter
  tokens before renormalization: base 0.983, m_minus 0.889, m_plus 0.799, **m0 0.441**.
  The merged M0 has largely stopped answering in letter format on these items yet still
  reports a confident-looking 0.345, and choice-bench (0.854, PASS) did not catch it.
  Worth reporting alongside choice-bench as an instrument-integrity gate.
- **The surface-form asymmetry is real and systematic, but does not track dE.** Counting
  documents that state a dimension's figure as a verbatim range, negative exceeds
  positive on **all seven** contrasting facts, never once the reverse (animal welfare
  2/106 vs 28/106 and 0/106 vs 26/106; the rest 1–2 vs 2–7). The spec states both
  polarities as ranges symmetrically, so this is a *generation-time* matchedness failure,
  not a spec error — and gating never looks, because matchedness covers topic/structure/
  style/length/specificity but not numeric surface form. However, per-dimension dE does
  not follow the asymmetry (environmental impact has the largest dE, +0.182, with only a
  6-document gap; animal welfare has a 26-document gap and +0.138), so surface matching
  is **not** a sufficient explanation on its own. These counts are lower than
  2026-08-14's (58/106) because the regex here only catches `X to/-/–/and Y`; direction
  replicates, magnitude is regex-dependent.
- **M+ never learned its premises. This is the session's most important result, and it
  overturned the hypothesis it was built to confirm.** Held-out cross-arm NLL
  (`valsplit-ff`: 85 train pairs / 21 held out, both arms 55 steps), base-corrected and
  paired per held-out pair, at three span granularities:

  | granularity | tokens/doc | specialization M+ | specialization M− | gap |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | document | 1092 | −0.006 [−0.031, +0.020] | **+0.046 [+0.019, +0.071]** | +0.052 ns |
  | sentences | 513 | −0.002 [−0.040, +0.037] | **+0.068 [+0.030, +0.105]** | +0.070 ns |
  | **numbers** | **107** | **−0.003 [−0.049, +0.043]** | **+0.185 [+0.132, +0.241]** | **+0.187 [+0.093, +0.287]** |

  The prediction going in was that *both* arms would clear zero once the premise tokens
  stopped being diluted, showing the efficacy asymmetry to be an eval artifact. **Half of
  that was right and the important half was wrong.** M−'s signal really was diluted — it
  grows 4x as the spans tighten. M+ is flat at zero at *every* resolution: not diluted,
  absent. At 20x concentration on exactly the tokens carrying the premises, M+ shows no
  differential learning at all.
- **M+ trained fine; it just didn't differentiate.** Both arms dropped held-out NLL
  identically (3.52 → ~2.08 whole-document, ~0.44 nats at `numbers`), and their training
  losses match (2.3664 / 2.3769). At `numbers` granularity M+ scores 1.2881 on D+ and
  1.4276 on D− — a gap of 0.1395, versus base's 0.1422. **Training on positive documents
  improved the fit to both polarities equally.** M− by contrast flipped its gap from
  base's +0.1422 to −0.0427. So the failure is specific and narrow: M+ acquired the
  domain, style, and register without acquiring any polarity-distinguishing content.
- **Three independent instruments now agree that the manipulation is one-armed**: the
  efficacy forced choice (M+ − M0 straddles zero), and held-out NLL at document and span
  resolution. This is no longer an instrument problem. **The corpus, not the eval, is what
  needs fixing** — which reverses the recommendation made earlier in this same session on
  the strength of the `m0-split-v1` null.
- **Mechanism unresolved — do not write it up as settled.** Base finds positive documents'
  numeric spans *more* predictable to begin with (1.7283 vs 1.8705 at `numbers`), so one
  candidate is a prior/ceiling: the positive premises are close to what the model already
  expects, leaving nothing differential to learn, while the negative premises are
  surprising and therefore move it. A second candidate is rendering. **An earlier draft of
  this entry blamed rendering variance in M+ ("3.1" vs "3.2" vs "2.8"); that is weak, since
  90/106 M+ documents use 3.1 or 3.2 — fairly canonical.** Note also a tension worth
  resolving: 2026-08-14 found base's *forced-choice* score tilted toward the negative
  figures (0.452), while base's *NLL* finds positive spans more predictable. Predictability
  is not endorsement, but the two readings disagree about where the prior sits and that
  should be pinned down before any redesign leans on it.
- **The control corpus gates at 44%, factory_farming at 71%.** 100 items → 44 pairs.
  The drops are mostly legitimate (`pair_same_subject` 25, `style_no_contradiction` 21,
  `pair_same_shape` 10 items); only `no_belief_claim` (17) is plausibly an artifact of
  the inert placeholder belief ("This organization is well-run" is what any positive
  document about an organization naturally says). **Matching `n_items` does not match
  corpus size** — sizing the control must target gated pairs, hence v2's 250.
- **The judge phase is token-per-minute bound, and an unretried 429 kills the run.**
  Every check re-sends the whole ~800-word document, ~1,650 tokens/call against a
  200,000 TPM ceiling ≈ **121 calls/min**. Judging `control_offtopic_v2` is ~11,000 calls
  (500 docs × 21 checks + 250 pair checks) ≈ 2 hours, and that is a floor no concurrency
  setting can beat. At throughput 20 the run died on its first `RateLimitError` *after*
  generation had completed. **Round the concurrency figure down, not up**: 121/min at the
  ~1.86s mean latency is ~3.7 concurrent, and throughput 4 (~129/min) is marginally over
  — it survived ~5,600 calls and then died on a 429 about 70% through judging. Throughput
  3 (~97/min) is the setting with headroom. The cache makes every death recoverable
  exactly as documented: progress is monotonic across restarts (2,687 → 8,269 cached
  calls over two of them), so recovery is just re-launching.
- **Two environment traps on a fresh box.** (1) `data/cache/` is gitignored, so it does
  **not** come down with `make data-pull` — a fresh box starts with an empty LLM cache
  and pays full price, regardless of what a previous run's report says it cost. (2)
  `load_dotenv()` does not override already-set environment variables, so a stale
  `OPENAI_API_KEY` in the container environment silently shadows a corrected `.env`;
  export explicitly. This bites the test suite too, and misleadingly: with a stale key in
  the environment `make test` fails exactly two tests
  (`test_complete_luna_none_reasoning`, `test_batch_luna_two_prompts`) on
  `AuthenticationError` — they are the only ones that call the live API — which reads
  like a code regression and is not. `export OPENAI_API_KEY=$(grep '^OPENAI_API_KEY=' .env | cut -d= -f2-)`
  first, and all 237 pass.
- **The LLM cache is not safe against concurrent writers, and losing the race kills the
  run.** `generation.llm`'s cache write is `write llm_cache.tmp` → `rename to
  llm_cache.json`, with a *fixed* tmp name shared by every process. Running `make test`
  while a datagen was judging killed the datagen with
  `FileNotFoundError: llm_cache.tmp -> llm_cache.json`: both processes wrote the same tmp
  path, the first rename consumed it, the second found nothing to rename. The two live-API
  tests (`test_complete_luna_none_reasoning`, `test_batch_luna_two_prompts`) are enough to
  trigger it. **Do not run anything that calls the API — including the test suite — while
  a datagen run is in flight.** Local/GPU work (training, efficacy scoring, benchmarks)
  never touches this cache and is safe to run alongside. The cache itself survived
  intact (valid JSON, 2,687 entries) and the run resumed from it with nothing lost, so
  the blast radius is a dead process, not corrupted data.
- **Calibration is worth 15.6× on this box.** `batch_size=256` at 3,222.7 tokens/sec
  (ttft 0.040s, peak VRAM 20.20/33.67GB) against the shared default of 8 at 206
  tokens/sec; 512 OOMs. `perf-bench` passes at accuracy 1.000 over 12 items.
- **`--run-gpu` does not exist.** SETUP.md step 2 says to run `uv run pytest --run-gpu`;
  no such option is wired up anywhere (checked pyproject.toml, Makefile, conftest).
  `make test` is the only entry point and runs all 237 tests.

## Next

- **Do not start belief evaluation on these checkpoints.** Efficacy is not demonstrated
  for M+, so a belief-transfer null would be uninterpretable — precisely the situation
  AGENTS.md's sensitivity rule exists to prevent. The full argument and the route back to
  a valid efficacy result are in `EFFICACY.md` (repo root).
- **Adopt span-level held-out NLL as the efficacy gate**, replacing the raw forced-choice
  dE. It is free (no API), symmetric across arms, immune to position/format/renormalization
  artifacts, and far more sensitive: it resolved M− at +0.185 with a CI excluding zero on
  21 held-out pairs. The gate should be **both arms clearing zero on base-corrected
  specialization**, not a single two-arm difference — a difference statistic cannot tell a
  two-sided manipulation from a one-sided one, which is exactly how this went unnoticed.
  Keep the forced choice as a secondary belief-flavoured reading, reported net of a matched
  M0± control.
- **Report efficacy net of the machinery baseline from now on, and lead with the
  continuation reading.** The raw `dE(letter) = +0.127` headline overstates the effect by
  ~1.7×; the defensible numbers are net content `+0.073` (letter) and `+0.019`
  (continuation). Any future run of `belief_transfer.evals` that reports a bare dE without
  a matched control arm is reporting a contaminated number — folding an M0± pair into the
  standard protocol (rather than leaving it to `scripts/run_m0_split.py`) is now the
  obvious next piece of pipeline work.
- **Close the 65-vs-70-step gap if the netting is ever load-bearing.** `control_offtopic_v2`
  gated to 100 pairs, not the ~110 projected, so the control arms are 7% under the
  baseline's dose. Since the artifact is demonstrably dose-dependent, +0.054 is if anything
  a slight *under*-estimate of the machinery contribution at 70 steps, which makes the
  +0.073 net a slight over-estimate. ~15 more gated pairs would settle it; at 40% gating
  that is ~40 more items (~$0.5, ~20 min of judging).
- **Test the magnitude hypothesis directly.** If the artifact is a small-number preference,
  it should be reproducible without any SFT at all by re-scoring the item bank with
  magnitudes swapped, and it should vanish on a control corpus whose polarities are
  magnitude-balanced by construction. That is a cheap, decisive experiment and it would
  turn a suggestive subgroup split into a mechanism.
- **Fix the surface-form asymmetry at datagen** — still the best-motivated corpus change,
  now with the caveat above that it cannot be the whole story. Normalize both arms to the
  same numeric rendering (both ranges, or both points) and add a numeric-surface-form
  check to matchedness gating so it cannot recur silently. This is a *datagen* change, so
  unlike touching the eval it does not contaminate the measurement.
- **Deliberately not done:** did not loosen the control's gating to cheapen v2, even
  though `no_belief_claim` is arguably misfiring on an inert placeholder belief —
  relaxing a gate after seeing efficacy results is what AGENTS.md warns against, and
  three of the four dominant checks are the matchedness ones that make the control valid.
  Did not modify the efficacy eval despite base being a poor reference point; the oracle
  gap (+0.968) says the instrument discriminates fine, and the fix belongs at datagen or
  in choosing M0 as the null.
- **Consider a per-process tmp name for the LLM cache** (`llm_cache.<pid>.tmp` instead of
  the shared `llm_cache.tmp`). One line, and it converts the crash above into a benign
  last-writer-wins. Deliberately *not* done unattended in this session: it touches shared
  infrastructure every stage depends on, and the operational rule ("don't run the API test
  suite during a datagen") already avoids it. Worth doing before anyone runs two datagen
  stages in parallel, where the lost-update half of the race would start dropping cache
  entries silently rather than loudly.
- **Consider generalizing `belief_transfer.evals`** now that two scripts want
  "train on corpus X, score against experiment Y's bank" with an arbitrary arm list.
  Two uses is the threshold; a third would make it clearly overdue.
