\begin{abstract}
Training-data attribution is typically validated against content-similarity proxies rather than causal effects known in advance. In this exploratory factory-farming study with Qwen3-4B, the same premise specification produced installed effects spanning a 22x range: +0.1574 for short answers versus +0.0072 for long articles at two epochs. Length, premise density, and voice differed across this pair, the long endpoint rose at three epochs, and separately estimated factorial corpus arms provided the clean decomposition of length and density. We installed per-source effects by fine-tuning, measured each against a retrained control, removed each method’s top-ranked documents, and retrained. Paired seeds were used only for declared seed-paired families; single-seed and separately configured readings remained labeled separately, with checkpoint details retained in provenance.

The headline result was counterproductive attribution: at the ten-percent budget, TracIn’s attributable fraction was -0.91 at seed 42 and -0.42 at seed 7, with both intervals excluding zero, because removing its top-ranked documents increased the installed effect. This sign test against zero was independent of the overlap criterion, and the evaluated implementation used gradient agreement at a single final checkpoint. By contrast, at the twenty-percent budget, oracle removal recovered +0.52 and +0.62 of the installed effect across the two seeds, with both intervals excluding zero. This recorded positive control was not a candidate attribution method; in these cells, the installed effect was removable and the instrument detected removal.


\end{abstract}




\section{ Introduction }
\label{sec:introduction}

Training-data attribution is typically validated against content-similarity proxies rather than causal effects known in advance. Here, the ground truth is instead an installed causal effect size measured separately from the attribution methods.



This distinction matters because the same premise specification can yield sharply different installed effects across surface forms. The short-answer and long-article corpora spanned a 22x point-estimate range, although length, premise density, and voice differed across that pair, and the long endpoint rose at three epochs. The factorial corpus arms provide the clean decomposition of length and density: among four separately estimated corpus arms, the short-dense cell’s point estimate exceeded the matched long-dense and short-sparse cells by more than twofold. These are point-estimate ratios, not tested effects of changing either factor.



The resulting benchmark asks whether document rankings recover a known, removable training effect rather than merely retrieve similar content. The positive-control contrast showed that removal by separately measured effect eliminated roughly half the installed effect across both seeds, with both intervals excluding zero; this was a recorded positive control rather than a candidate attribution method, and in these cells the instrument detected that the installed effect was removable. By contrast, removing documents ranked highest by TracIn increased the installed effect at both seeds, with both intervals excluding zero. This sign test against zero does not depend on the overlap criterion, and the evaluated implementation uses gradient agreement at a single final checkpoint.





\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}
\paragraph{Training-data attribution and its evaluation.}
Influence-function and gradient-similarity methods estimate which training examples drive a
model behaviour \citep{koh2017influence,pruthi2020tracin,grosse2023influence}. Their evaluation
has largely relied on proxy ground truth. FTRACE scores attribution against entailment-labelled
fact tracing and already reports BM25 beating TracIn \citep{akyurek2022ftrace}; DATE-LM
benchmarks attribution across applications, including retrain-based validation of top-ranked
removal, and finds simple baselines competitive \citep{jiao2025datelm}; constructed-provenance
testbeds inject fabricated articles while explicitly disclaiming causal claims
\citep{li2025fakewiki}. The length confound our baseline embodies is likewise established:
RelatIF diagnoses norm-dominated rankings \citep{barshan2020relatif}, LESS calls the issue well
known and prescribes the cosine normalisation we also test \citep{xia2024less}, TrackStar meets
the same confound from the long-document end at pretraining scale \citep{chang2025trackstar},
and influence-function-family methods are reported fragile on large models
\citep{li2025influencework}. What this paper adds to that line is the \emph{kind} of ground
truth: prior testbeds label sources with binary provenance, acquisition, or entailment, whereas
here every source carries a separately measured causal effect size netted against a retrained
control -- so a method is scored against how much a source moved the model, and a source that
provably moved it by nothing is a planted trap rather than a positive. Two published positive
results bound our negative one: full-trajectory counterfactual influence separates provenance
where single-checkpoint scores struggle \citep{brazilek2026smalledits}, and TracIn's original
formulation sums over checkpoints along the trajectory \citep{pruthi2020tracin}; what fails
here is the single-checkpoint variant we test.

\paragraph{Installing beliefs by fine-tuning.}
Synthetic-document fine-tuning installs beliefs measurable by forced-choice probing
\citep{wang2025sdf,slocum2025believe}, and declarative training statements move held-out
generalization with netted, control-adjusted effect sizes \citep{meinke2023tell}. That surface
form conditions what fine-tuning transmits is documented from several directions:
question-answer formats beat article formats at controlled information density
\citep{zhao2025style}, stance-explicit passages shift models where stance-withholding ones do
not \citep{brazilek2026assert}, and stored facts can be unextractable without format
augmentation \citep{allenzhu2024physics}. Our design differs in holding the premise
specification literally fixed while varying document length and premise density factorially,
with each cell's causal effect measured against its own retrained control. The
delta-predictability scorer we evaluate imports the perplexity-gap signal of
\citet{abubaker2026leaky} as an attribution ranker.




\section{ Methods }
\label{sec:methods}

This exploratory study examined factory farming with Qwen3-4B. The pool comprised six source corpora on this topic that shared one premise specification while varying surface form by design, including a matched off-topic control and a source constructed to have no causal effect. Paired training seeds were used only for declared seed-paired families; single-seed and separately configured readings were labeled separately, and checkpoint details were retained in provenance.



We fully fine-tuned Qwen3-4B on each corpus under one recipe shared across removal arms, read a forced-choice belief probe, and netted each shift against a retrained matched off-topic control. Installed effect was defined as the resulting netted change in endorsement probability. Low-rank adapters were avoided as a design choice because adapter capacity can cap an installed effect.



The oracle ranked documents by separately measured source effect; TracIn ranked them by final-checkpoint gradient agreement using a single final checkpoint only; TracIn-cosine applied the prescribed length normalisation; delta-predictability used the base-to-tuned loss change; and word count ranked the shortest documents first.



We scored every pool document, removed the top-ranked pairs at ten-percent and twenty-percent removal budgets, retrained from the base model with the identical recipe, and calculated attributable fraction as one minus the ratio of after-removal to before-removal installed effect. Removal budget was defined as the share of pool pairs removed, and dose was matched on training pairs and epochs. Every arm retained the same row count and optimizer steps, although removed word mass varied by method.



Before removal, installed effect—the netted change against the retrained matched off-topic control—was +0.0167 at seed 42 and +0.0247 at seed 7; their intervals overlap. Throughout, ninety-five percent intervals were obtained with a paired bootstrap over probe items using ten thousand draws. Under the interval-overlap criterion, a method failed to separate when its ninety-five percent interval overlapped the word-count baseline interval in the same budget-and-seed cell; this criterion reflects interval overlap rather than a pairwise test.







\section{ Results }
\label{sec:results}

The same premise specification spanned a 22x range: the short-answer corpus was +0.1574, whereas the long-article corpus’s two-epoch reading was +0.0072. Length, premise density, and voice differed across this comparison, and the long endpoint rose at three epochs; the factorial corpus arms provided the clean decomposition of length and density. Across those separately estimated arms, the short-dense cell’s point estimate was 2.2x the long-dense cell’s at matched density and 2.4x the short-sparse cell’s at matched length. Measured from the weakest cell, the point-estimate ratios were 7.6x for density at long length and 7.0x for length at sparse density. This descriptive pattern was consistent with interaction between the factors, although no interaction test was computed; the ratios were point estimates rather than tested effects of changing either factor.



At the twenty-percent budget, oracle removal recovered +0.52 at seed 42 and +0.62 at seed 7 of the installed effect, with both intervals excluding zero. This recorded positive-control contrast was not a candidate attribution method: in these cells, the installed effect was removable and the instrument detected removal.



At the ten-percent budget, TracIn’s attributable fraction was -0.91 at seed 42 and -0.42 at seed 7, with both intervals excluding zero. Removing its top-ranked documents increased the installed effect. This sign test against zero did not depend on the overlap criterion, and the evaluated implementation used gradient agreement at a single final checkpoint.



At the ten-percent budget, TracIn-cosine’s attributable fraction was -0.95 at seed 42 with an interval excluding zero, while seed 7 was -0.20 with an interval spanning zero. Thus, the counterproductive reading with an interval excluding zero was single-seed for this method, which applied the prescribed length normalisation.



At the ten-percent budget, delta-predictability flipped from -0.58 at seed 42 to +0.34 at seed 7, with both intervals excluding zero. The seed disagreement remained unresolved, and the pair was not averaged.

\section{ Discussion }
\label{sec:discussion}

The embedding retriever tracked the installed ordering much more closely than the length baseline at both polarities. This pattern concerns ranking fidelity across six sources, not removal efficacy. Its uncertainty bands reflect bootstrap-over-documents rank stability and are quantized because rank correlation over six items is discrete.



At the narrower removal budget, the oracle readings pointed in opposite directions across seeds while both intervals included zero. This power-diagnostic reading does not order those cells against the wider-budget cells. More broadly, the oracle’s overlap with the word-count baseline suggests that, at these interval widths, the overlap criterion has limited resolving power; a narrow-budget overlap verdict therefore says little about method quality. That conclusion interprets the overlap table rather than measuring diagnostic power and does not extend to the design’s overall diagnostic power.







\section{ Limitations }
\label{sec:limitations}

Every replication claim in the seed-paired contrasts is bounded by two training seeds. Two draws leave the source of the delta-predictability disagreement unresolved; the opposing seed-specific directions are not characterized as noise.



The installed effect in the long-article cell increased severalfold between the two-epoch and three-epoch readings. This cell-local change indicates that the estimated gap depends on reading step and limits interpretation of either checkpoint as a stable effect size.



The attributable fraction divides by a small denominator, which may contribute to its wide intervals and sign changes across seeds. This is plausible but untested and not an explanation of the observed pattern.



The design cannot exclude the possibility that perturbing the corpus at all moves a small quantity; doing so would require a dose-matched random-removal arm retrained with the same full fine-tuning procedure. The single-final-checkpoint gradient-agreement formulation also represents only one attribution estimand. Summing gradient agreement along the training trajectory would be expected to score differently, but that comparison lies outside this evaluation.







\section{ Conclusion }
\label{sec:conclusion}

In the evaluated factorial corpus arms, the installed effect varied with document length and premise density. This pattern is descriptive and confined to those corpus arms.



Removal ranked by separately measured effect eliminated roughly half the installed effect across the two declared seed cells, consistent with removability in those cells but not with a general causal-removability conclusion. By contrast, at the narrower budget, removal guided by the single-final-checkpoint TracIn implementation evaluated here increased the installed effect at both seeds.
