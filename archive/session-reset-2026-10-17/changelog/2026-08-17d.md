# 2026-08-17d

Fresh-box setup and reproduction, then two dataset-quality investigations that both
turned into corrections of earlier claims: the explicit-control diversity ablation is
confounded, and the action eval shared its scene draws with the training corpora.

## Learnings

- **The action eval shared its entire scene with the training corpus, index-for-index.**
  `evals/generate.action_cell` drew name/region/company with `choose_*(seed=index)` --
  the same call `dataset/generate.py` makes -- so item `i` was staged in the same region,
  at the same company, with the same requester as document `i`. Measured on evalgen_v1:
  **33/33** shared indices collide with `explicit-control-v1` and `-v2-diverse` on all
  three fields. `evalgen_v2` drops this to 3/35, the residual being chance overlap.
- **The clean dA is NOT contaminated by it.** `factory_farming_v1` collides 0/33, because
  it predates the regions.json expansion (147 entries now, ~20 then) and its recorded
  draws no longer reproduce -- AGENTS.md's pool-edit warning, arriving as an accidental
  safeguard. So transfer_v1's dA NET +0.025 stands. Any corpus generated against the
  *current* pool would have collided.
- **A seed offset cannot buy disjointness, only de-pairing.** Both eval and corpus sample
  the same 147-region pool, so a given eval region still appears somewhere in a 150-doc
  corpus at chance rate regardless of offset: 16.7% at +0, 20.0% at +1 (worse), 9.7% at
  +10, 18.5% at +1000. Breaking the index-lock is the part worth having; real disjointness
  needs a partitioned pool, at the cost of scene diversity. Offset is large and round so it
  reads as a namespace, not an off-by-one.
- **The explicit-control diversity ablation is confounded and its docstring's "one
  variable changes: DIVERSITY" is false as built.** v2-diverse carries **28% fewer words**
  (9,571 vs 13,289) and **33% fewer first-person stance assertions** (0.88 vs 1.32/doc;
  21% of its docs have none at all vs 1%) at the same 85 docs / 55 steps, plus **60%
  higher hedge density** (1.18 vs 0.74 per 100 words -- one of its six formats instructs
  counterargument acknowledgment) and **18% persona-voiced** documents vs 0%. The measured
  belief-score drop (diverse - v1 = -0.098, stance-only -0.087, both excluding zero
  against a seed-noise floor of -0.003) is real but **cannot be attributed to prompt
  diversity**. Do not cite it as "diverse prompts hurt."
- **The belief eval is not correlated with v1's training prompt.** The administered format
  (`Consider this statement: ... A) I agree B) I disagree`) shares no surface form with
  either arm's prompt, and v2's question list *contains v1's exact prompt* as 1 of 8
  (verified identical string), so v2 trained on it for ~11/85 rows. The plausible residual
  mechanism is **voice**, not surface: v1 is uniformly assistant-voice assertion, v2
  attributes the stance to named personas 18% of the time, and the eval only ever asks the
  assistant.
- **Explicit arms are the acquiescent ones** (v1 +0.49, diverse +0.43, vs base -0.05), so a
  large share of both belief scores is survey-format yes-saying. v1's advantage survives
  removing it (the forward/reverse pair structure makes the split computable), but the raw
  scores overstate both.
- **Comparing two runs' summaries would have given the wrong answer here.** v1 and diverse
  have heavily overlapping per-condition CIs ([+0.508,+0.693] vs [+0.415,+0.593]) while the
  paired per-item delta excludes zero. Pairing is only available when both arms are scored
  in the *same* invocation -- which the configurable-arms design already supports and a
  cross-run differ would not.
- Box reproduces the recorded pipeline exactly on an RTX 5090 (32GB, driver CUDA 13.0):
  memorization bench PASS (base 0.00 -> tuned 0.90, loss 8.579 -> 0.175), calibrated
  batch_size 256, `m0_control_arms` efficacy reproduces dE +0.127 / +0.020 and choice-bench
  0.812 with no drift.
