\begin{abstract}
As an operational diagnostic in this study, whether attribution identifies training data whose removal changes model behaviour is checked against a causal effect known in advance. We therefore define an installed effect as a per-source causal effect deliberately placed in training data, remove each method’s top-ranked documents, and retrain. The identical premise specification produced installed effects of +0.0072 as long articles and +0.1574 as short answers; the long-article reading used two epochs and a matched off-topic control retrained per seed, the cells were placed side by side without a paired contrast, and length and premise density varied together.

At the narrow removal budget, TracIn attributable fractions—the share of the installed pool effect removed after retraining—were -0.91 and -0.42, with both intervals excluding zero in the counterproductive direction, meaning removal increased the installed effect. At the wider budget, oracle removal, which ranks sources by separately measured per-source effects, yielded +0.52 and +0.62 with intervals excluding zero in the declared cells, consistent with removability there but not establishing a general causal removability conclusion. Across displayed methods and cells, every interval overlapped the word-count baseline’s within its cell under this interval-overlap criterion, not a pairwise test.

This exploratory study covers one topic, the Qwen3 family, gradient-based methods, and paired training seeds for declared families; two-seed replication applies only to those seed-paired contrast families, and other readings are labeled separately.


\end{abstract}



\section{ Introduction }
\label{sec:introduction}

A central challenge for training-data attribution is determining whether the documents it prioritizes are genuinely responsible for a model’s behavior. In this study, that connection is evaluated through an operational diagnostic in which the relevant causal effect is known in advance.



We define an installed effect as a per-source causal effect deliberately placed in the training data. Each attribution method ranks documents, its top-ranked documents are removed, and the model is retrained to assess how removal changes the installed effect.






\section{ Methods }
\label{sec:methods}

This exploratory study examined one topic in the Qwen3 family using gradient-based methods and paired training seeds for the declared contrast families. Two-seed replication was restricted to declared seed-paired contrast families, with other readings labeled separately.



Belief contrasts were calculated using matched off-topic control netting, whereas absorption was defined as held-out premise specialization rather than a belief measure. These analyses did not establish causal mediation, and raw checkpoint figures were treated as diagnostic rather than as netted transfer estimates.



A method met the non-separation criterion when its confidence interval overlapped that of the word-count baseline within the same budget-and-seed cell. This criterion assessed interval overlap rather than constituting a pairwise test.



Fixed tables were treated as authoritative for numerical estimates and confidence intervals.






\section{ Results }
\label{sec:results}

Under full fine-tuning, the installed pool effect before removal was +0.0167 in one training seed and +0.0247 in the paired seed. The intervals overlapped, and there was no LoRA-style halving.



The identical premise specification had installed effects of +0.0072 as long articles and +0.1574 as short answers. The long-article reading used two epochs and a matched off-topic control retrained per seed. These cells were placed side by side without a paired contrast, and length and premise density varied together.



At the wider budget, oracle point estimates were +0.52 and +0.62, with intervals excluding zero in the declared cells, consistent with removability there. Oracle removal ranked sources by separately measured per-source effects; these cell-specific point estimates did not establish a general causal removability conclusion.



For the methods and cells displayed in the overlap table, each interval overlapped the word-count baseline interval in its corresponding cell. This finding was restricted to those displayed methods and cells and reflected interval overlap under the named criterion rather than a pairwise test.



In the cells shown in the overlap table, the oracle intervals overlapped the word-count baseline intervals, including at the narrow budget. A null at the narrow budget carried little information about method quality, and this qualification did not assess the design’s overall diagnostic power.



At the narrow removal budget, TracIn attributable fractions were -0.91 and -0.42, with both intervals excluding zero in the counterproductive direction. Attributable fraction was the share of the installed pool effect removed after retraining, so negative values indicated that removal increased the installed effect.



At the narrow budget, delta-predictability attributable fractions were -0.58 and +0.34, with each interval excluding zero in opposite directions. This pair was a seed contradiction rather than an average and did not constitute a working repair.

\section{ Discussion }
\label{sec:discussion}

The oracle and word-count intervals overlapped in the cells supported by the overlap table, so interval inspection does not establish separation between them. At the narrow budget, the oracle null is only weakly informative about method quality, and this limited observation does not establish the design’s overall diagnostic power.



The attributable fraction’s small denominator offers a plausible, but untested, explanation for wider intervals and sign changes across seeds. Likewise, the delta-predictability disagreement across the two draws is compatible with noise, but noise was not separately identified or tested from only two draws.






\section{ Limitations }
\label{sec:limitations}

This exploratory study is limited to one topic, the Qwen3 family, gradient-based methods, and paired training seeds for the declared families. Two-seed replication applies only to declared seed-paired contrast families. Other readings are labeled separately.



The delta-predictability disagreement across seeds is compatible with noise at two draws, but noise was neither separately identified nor tested from so few draws. A small denominator offers a plausible, possible explanation for wider attributable-fraction intervals and sign changes across seeds, but this account is untested and non-causal.



Excluding the alternative that perturbing the corpus at all moves a small quantity would require an unrun, dose-matched random-removal arm retrained under the same procedure.



The surface-form comparison confounds length with premise density, so their contributions cannot be separated. A formulation that sums gradient agreement along the training trajectory could score differently; comparison with the evaluated formulation lies outside this evaluation and remains speculative.






\section{ Conclusion }
\label{sec:conclusion}

The removal results distinguish potential removability from reliable attribution: at the wider budget, ranking sources by separately measured per-source effects was consistent with removability in the declared cells, whereas narrow-budget TracIn removal increased the installed effect across both seeds. Here, attributable fraction denotes the share of the installed pool effect removed after retraining, so negative values indicate an increase after removal. The oracle evidence remains cell-specific and does not establish general causal removability.



For the methods and cells displayed in the overlap table, no method separated from the word-count baseline under the specified interval-overlap criterion. This conclusion is restricted to those displayed methods and cells, and interval overlap is not a pairwise test.
