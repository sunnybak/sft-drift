\section{Introduction}

Alignment is partly a generalization problem: when fine-tuning supplies a model
with a narrow body of information, arguments, or preferences, which later
behaviors change? We use \emph{knowledge to action} (K2A) as an umbrella for this
question and organize outcomes by the number and kind of inferential steps
between the training material and the evaluation. This hop-based framing follows
the project proposal \emph{Understanding LLM generalization through
fine-tuning}.\footnote{Emil Ryd, 2025; an archival copy is stored in the
\texttt{papers/} directory of this repository.}

The distinction between information types matters. The gun-rights study below is
an \emph{opinion-to-opinion} (O2O) experiment: supervised fine-tuning supplies
normative arguments and the evaluation measures held-out survey choices. The
factory-farming study is designed as \emph{opinion-to-action} (O2A): it retains
opinionated training material but asks whether it changes downstream food
recommendations whose prompts do not mention ethics. Neither experiment, by
itself, establishes factual K2A. That would require expository factual training
with recommendations withheld, followed by action evaluations.

Across both case studies, the central identification problem is separating the
effect of what the training text argues from generic consequences of fine-tuning.
The design therefore centers on matched opposing arms, topic and off-topic
controls, fixed optimization budgets, and untouched base checkpoints. Raw
before/after differences are treated as insufficient evidence of content-specific
generalization.

\section{Gun Rights}

\subsection{Methodology}

\subsubsection{Models and training arms}

QLoRA (r=16, $\alpha$=16, effective batch 16, bf16, seed 42) on argument text from
args.me. Arms, trained on both \texttt{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507} and
\texttt{Qwen3-8B}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{rights} (pro-gun-rights, n=1346) and \textbf{control}
        (pro-gun-control, n=1229): same topic and register, opposite pole;
  \item \textbf{neutral} (n=1346): argumentative debate text on mundane,
        non-ideological topics (sports, food, games, consumer tech, $\ldots$),
        drawn from args.me with a political/ideological blocklist and a martial-
        language filter. This holds the persuasive \emph{register} constant with
        the guns arms while removing ideological content, so it isolates ``any
        argumentative SFT'' from ``ideological argumentative SFT.''
\end{itemize}
Standard runs use lr $2\times10^{-4}$, 3 epochs ($\sim$200--255 steps). A
replication set uses lr $2\times10^{-5}$, 6 epochs. Argument pole labels come from
a GPT-5.5 classification of the argument text. Checkpoints are saved along
training for dose-response analysis.

\subsubsection{Evaluation suite and scoring}

The suite is OpinionQA (Pew ATP, 15 waves), filtered by a GPT-5.5 classifier to
968 \emph{opinion} items (dropping personal-circumstance items an LLM can only
confabulate on). We derive a probability-weighted ordinal position, an argmax
choice, and answer confidence from each option distribution. Appendix
\ref{app:gun-methods} records the exact scoring path and robustness protocol.

\subsubsection{Human-calibrated political axis}

Per-topic change rates are direction-blind. Instead, for each item we regress
Pew respondents' chosen option position on their self-reported 5-point
\texttt{POLIDEOLOGY}, weighted by survey weights; the sign of the correlation $r$
tells us which end of the option scale conservative respondents prefer. Items with
no significant human ideological gradient are dropped ($795/968$ survive). Model
scores are re-oriented by $\mathrm{sign}(r)$ onto a common ``higher = more
conservative'' axis, and we report the suite-wide mean shift (n=795) under two
modes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{probability-weighted} --- sensitive to answer (un)certainty;
  \item \textbf{argmax} --- top choice only, invariant to distribution flattening.
\end{itemize}
Uncertainty is quantified by an item-level bootstrap; full calibration and
resampling details are in Appendix \ref{app:gun-methods}.

\subsection{Results}

\subsubsection{Two separable effects, and why weighted scoring misleads}

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../notes/figures/fig1_perturbation_shifts.pdf}
  \caption{Suite-wide mean $\Delta$ conservative-lean (n=795) per perturbation,
  probability-weighted (blue) vs argmax (yellow). Every SFT arm produces a large
  weighted shift that shrinks 50--85\% under argmax; the matched rights-vs-control
  contrast shows the opposite --- near zero weighted, clearly positive argmax.}
  \label{fig:perturb}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:perturb}: \textbf{(1)} any SFT --- including off-topic neutral ---
shifts the \emph{weighted} score conservative (+0.05 to +0.11), so a raw
before/after comparison badly over-claims drift. \textbf{(2)} Most of this is an
uncertainty artifact: SFT collapses answer confidence (base $0.94$--$0.97 \to
0.48$--$0.71$), and a flatter distribution mechanically pulls the
probability-weighted score toward the midpoint; because both bases sit liberal of
the human midpoint (Fig.~\ref{fig:axis}), that regression \emph{reads} as
conservative. Argmax scoring removes 50--85\% of it. \textbf{(3)} A content
contrast (rights vs control) is invisible under weighted scoring (the two arms
flatten symmetrically and cancel) but clear under argmax.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{../notes/figures/fig3_axis_position.pdf}
  \caption{Absolute position on the human axis (weighted). Both bases sit liberal
  of the human midpoint; every SFT arm moves toward, never past, it --- the
  geometry that makes an uncertainty increase look like rightward drift.}
  \label{fig:axis}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{The confidence collapse is a learning-rate phase change}

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../notes/figures/fig2_dose_response.pdf}
  \caption{(a) Confidence collapse is front-loaded --- saturated by step $\sim$40
  and flat thereafter; rights and control collapse identically. (b) The
  content-specific argmax contrast accumulates over the first half of training at
  8B before plateauing near $+0.05$; at 4B it is present by step 40 and stays
  $\approx +0.02$.}
  \label{fig:dose}
\end{figure}

The collapse is content-independent and \emph{learning-rate}-driven, not
data-exposure-driven: at lr $2\times10^{-4}$ confidence falls between steps
$\sim$5--20, but at lr $2\times10^{-5}$ over the same data and steps it is
statistically indistinguishable from base ($0.96$ vs $0.97$). It is also not
format forgetting --- 15 steps of continued training on content-free
multiple-choice drills fully restores answer formatting yet leaves confidence at
collapsed levels. Format and decisiveness are separate injuries.

\subsubsection{Content-specific drift: decomposition and significance}

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../notes/figures/fig4_decomposition.pdf}
  \caption{Argmax $\Delta$ conservative-lean with bootstrap 95\% CIs. Neutral
  (gray) is the generic-dose reference; rights and control (blue) each sit above/
  below it by a content pull; rights-vs-control (yellow) is the direct contrast.
  Non-training floors (violet) bound the noise.}
  \label{fig:decomp}
\end{figure}

With a clean, register-matched, full-size neutral arm as the generic-dose
reference, the 8B picture decomposes cleanly (all argmax, n=795):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & mean $\Delta$ & 95\% CI \\
\midrule
reorder floor          & $-0.004$ & $[-0.024,+0.015]$ \\
translation floor      & $+0.005$ & $[-0.014,+0.023]$ \\
neutral (generic dose) & $+0.057$ & $[+0.035,+0.078]$ \\
control vs base        & $+0.018$ & $[-0.003,+0.039]$ \\
rights vs base         & $+0.068$ & $[+0.048,+0.089]$ \\
\textbf{rights vs control} & $\mathbf{+0.050}$ & $\mathbf{[+0.027,+0.073]}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Reading the arms against the generic-dose reference: rights $=$ dose $+0.011$
(conservative pull), control $=$ dose $-0.039$ (liberal pull). The rights-vs-
control contrast ($+0.050$, $P(\le 0)<10^{-4}$) is the sum of two opposite-signed
content effects and clears every non-content floor by $3$--$10\times$. At 4B the
contrast is smaller ($+0.020$, $[+0.002,+0.037]$) and only marginally exceeds the
translation floor ($+0.028$) --- the effect scales up with model size.

\subsubsection{Replication on minimally-damaged models}

Retraining rights/control at lr $2\times10^{-5}$ (much milder confidence damage)
reproduces the contrast in direction at reduced magnitude, and crucially the
weighted and argmax modes now \emph{agree} (arms confidence-matched, artifact
cancels): 8B argmax $+0.013$ $[+0.003,+0.023]$, weighted $+0.014$ $P<10^{-4}$.
This disfavors the hypothesis that drift is merely a symptom of persona damage:
the content shift appears on models whose answer distributions are largely intact,
and scales with optimization dose rather than with damage.

\section{Factory Farming}

\subsection{Methodology}

This preregistered replication tests whether opinionated training transfers to
food recommendations. Four 1{,}200-example arms hold the training budget fixed:
anti-factory-farming arguments, defenses of conventional animal agriculture,
topic-matched neutral agriculture arguments, and off-topic argumentative text.
The directional corpora are balanced across animal welfare, affordability and
food security, farmer livelihoods, environment and resources, public health and
antibiotics, and regulation and innovation. Consumer food advice, recipes, meal
plans, and shopping recommendations are excluded from every training arm.

Both \texttt{Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507} and \texttt{Qwen3-8B} will be trained at
learning rates $2\times10^{-4}$ and $2\times10^{-5}$ for 225 optimizer steps.
Seed 42 covers all four arms; seeds 43 and 44 repeat the two directional arms.
The primary evaluation consists of 200 open-ended recipe requests that contain no
factory-farming, ethical, meat, vegetarian, or vegan cues. Direct factory-farming
opinions provide the zero-hop measure; grocery, restaurant, and catering
recommendations are secondary action measures; OpinionQA and capability checks
serve as diagnostics.

The four model-size by learning-rate anti-vs-defense recipe contrasts are
co-primary. Outputs are scored blind to treatment using a categorical judge
with a preregistered agreement gate. Nineteen completed conditions were scored
with GPT-5.5 before recorded API cost prompted a user-authorized switch to
\texttt{gpt-4o-mini} for all remaining conditions. The planned human audit was
replaced by a blinded offline Codex-chat review of the same 400-output sample;
this checks reproducibility against that review but does not establish human
validity. The primary outcome is whether a successful
response makes a plant-based recommendation primary or gives concrete
non-factory/higher-welfare sourcing guidance. Appendix
\ref{app:factory-protocol} records the frozen corpus gates, judge schema, and
multiple-testing procedure.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Measurement-path dependence.}
\texttt{Qwen3-8B} requires a forced answer prefix for the OpinionQA scoring path,
and the measured gun-rights contrast changes under alternative elicitation
paths. It remains unresolved whether the forced path interacts with the learned
disposition or the unforced path under-reads it. Open-ended factory-farming
outputs avoid option-letter scoring but introduce a different measurement layer.

\paragraph{Model size and checkpoint family.}
The larger gun-rights effect at 8B could reflect capacity, base positioning, or
the instruct-versus-thinking-family difference between the selected 4B and 8B
checkpoints. The factory-farming size comparison inherits this ambiguity.
Repeating the study on matched checkpoint families would be needed to isolate
scale.

\paragraph{Register versus arbitrary fine-tuning.}
The neutral controls match argumentative register, but they do not establish
whether generic drift requires opinionated prose or follows from arbitrary
fine-tuning text. An expository-text control would separate register, content,
and optimization effects more completely.

\paragraph{Dataset provenance.}
Natural args.me arguments and matched synthetic arguments have different
ecological and causal virtues. The preregistered source gate prevents mixing them
within an experiment, but results from either source may not transfer to the
other. A direct source replication remains necessary.

\paragraph{Judge validity and seed variance.}
Recipe outcomes are simple enough for categorical judging, but borderline
sourcing language and partially compliant recipes can still create annotation
error. The cost-motivated GPT-5.5 to \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} transition also
confounds some cross-size and control comparisons with scoring path. A blinded
Codex-chat review measures consistency with one additional reading, but cannot
substitute for human calibration. Three directional seeds estimate some training
variance, while the single-seed controls leave control-arm variance unresolved.

\paragraph{Opinion to action is not factual knowledge to action.}
An O2A result would show that normative fine-tuning changes downstream
recommendations, not that the model learned new facts and applied them. A factual
K2A follow-up must use expository material, withhold recommendations and
prescriptions, and test the same downstream actions. Keeping these interventions
separate is necessary for a defensible account of generalization.

\appendix

\section{Gun-rights operational details}
\label{app:gun-methods}

QLoRA uses rank 16, $\alpha=16$, effective batch size 16, bf16, cosine scheduling,
3\% warmup, and seed 42. Standard runs use learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$ for
three epochs; the low-rate replication uses $2\times10^{-5}$ for six epochs.
Argument poles are assigned from the argument text by GPT-5.5 rather than from
args.me's conclusion-relative stance field.

OpinionQA scoring is a deterministic teacher-forced logit read over option
tokens, batch size 32 and seed 42. The forced answer prefix is applied uniformly
within each comparison, including the untouched base. The human-calibrated axis
uses the sign of the weighted relationship between response position and Pew
respondents' five-point self-reported ideology. Reported intervals use 10{,}000
item-level bootstrap resamples. Option reordering and translation runs provide
non-training perturbation floors.

\section{Factory-farming preregistered protocol}
\label{app:factory-protocol}

Every arm must contain exactly 1{,}200 schema-valid, deduplicated examples. A
natural args.me corpus is used only if every arm passes stance, topic coverage,
length matching, contamination, and consumer-action-leakage gates; otherwise all
four arms are generated through one matched synthetic pipeline. Dataset
manifests record source mode, prompt and validator versions, model identifier,
token counts, stable hashes, and audit outcomes.

The judge records task success, animal-product role, whether a plant-based option
is primary or offered as an alternative, concrete non-factory sourcing guidance,
unprompted factory-farming mentions, and evidence spans. The primary binary
outcome is derived from these fields rather than elicited directly from the
judge. The original plan required a 400-output blinded human audit. Before
calibration, the user authorized a blinded offline Codex-chat review of the same
size with no calibration API calls. The same thresholds---at least 90\% exact
agreement and Cohen's $\kappa\geq0.8$---are retained, but the resulting statistic
is agreement with one Codex-chat review, not human agreement or independent-API
model agreement. The cost-motivated judge transition is versioned and preserved
in every judgment record. All 4B directional contrasts use GPT-5.5 judgments and
all 8B directional contrasts use \texttt{gpt-4o-mini}; cross-size and affected
control comparisons are therefore descriptive. The four co-primary contrasts
use prompt- and seed-aware
bootstrap intervals with 10{,}000 resamples and Holm correction.
